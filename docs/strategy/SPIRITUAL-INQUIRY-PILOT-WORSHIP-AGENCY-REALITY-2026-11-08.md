## Why this inquiry arose

The user proposes subjecting biblical and religious claims to ordinary standards of reasoning and verification, rather than presuming God's existence because Scripture or inherited tradition asserts it. The user asks whether worship, despite possible benefits, may implicitly condition a person or society to expect divine rescue instead of taking responsibility, adapting to reality and investigating natural and institutional causes. Related concerns: over-spiritualization, guilt, emotional restraint, passivity, distorted causal attributions, and religious influence on collective development. Do not interpret this as a declaration of disbelief or as a settled claim that religion causes underdevelopment.

## Provisional governing question

**Under what conditions does worship strengthen human responsibility, discernment and adaptation, and under what conditions might it encourage dependency, avoidance, excessive guilt or reduced attention to evidence?**

## Scope and testable subquestions

1. Distinguish God-existence claims, Scripture's evidentiary authority, and empirical psychological/social effects of worship. Neither useful effects prove God nor harmful effects disprove God.
2. Define agency, perceived locus of control, implicit religious conditioning, passive versus active religious coping, spiritual bypassing, scrupulosity (clinical claims require qualified sources), magical thinking, external attribution, authority dependence, and cognitive rigidity without collapsing distinct concepts.
3. Explore causal alternatives and confounders: socioeconomic conditions, education, trauma, governance, institutions, doctrinal differences, and reverse causality.
4. Explore positive pathways: meaningful action, courage, solidarity, institution building, self-regulation and moral accountability.
5. Examine biblical passages in historical/interpretive context as claims and arguments, without taking their truth for granted in cross-worldview inquiry.
6. Seek empirical measures and credible research before making general or country-level assertions; distinguish philosophical argument, empirical result, scriptural interpretation, and personal reflection.
7. Propose falsifiable predictions and contrary evidence, plus practical cases in work, health, politics and institutional response.
