## Service definitions

### AI Engineering
**Client problems:** inaccessible information, repetitive knowledge tasks, inconsistent AI outputs, or a need to evaluate whether an AI application is reliable enough for a specific use.
**Potential deliverables:** use-case assessment; proof of concept; retrieval-augmented application; bounded agent workflow; evaluation dataset and test report; deployment and handover documentation.
**Boundaries:** avoid claims of autonomous reliability, production readiness or guaranteed outcomes before testing.

### Intelligent Automation
**Client problems:** repeated manual transfers, fragmented tools, slow approvals, or unclear operational handoffs.
**Potential deliverables:** workflow map; integration design; pilot automation; exception handling and human approval points; runbook; monitoring recommendations.
**Boundaries:** access to client systems and sensitive data requires explicit authorization and appropriate controls.

### Knowledge Architecture
**Client problems:** scattered documents, inconsistent terminology, poor retrieval, unclear ownership of knowledge and decisions.
**Potential deliverables:** information architecture; taxonomy/ontology; structured repository; retrieval workflow; governance and maintenance guidance.
**Boundaries:** distinguish a designed structure from verified organizational adoption.

### Research & Advisory
**Client problems:** an unclear research question, a difficult decision, fragmented evidence, uncertain data quality or a proposed solution lacking a defensible basis.
**Potential deliverables:** research scoping; literature/evidence review; data analysis; methods and instrument support; problem diagnosis; technical feasibility assessment; decision memorandum.
**Boundaries:** respect research ethics, attribution, client confidentiality and academic integrity. Do not promise favorable results or offer misrepresentation of authorship.

## Engagement sequence
1. **Discovery:** understand the question, intended users, constraints, timeline and available information.
2. **Scope:** agree deliverables, assumptions, dependencies, acceptance criteria, fees and confidentiality requirements.
3. **Execution:** research, architect, build or analyze with checkpoints proportionate to risk.
4. **Verification:** review evidence, test outputs, identify limitations and document unresolved issues.
5. **Handover:** deliver agreed artifacts, operating guidance and recommendations for next steps.

## Public proof policy
- Only publish actual projects with verified facts and permission where needed.
- For internal demonstrations, label clearly **Independent demonstration** or **Concept prototype**, never as a client case study.
- Record problem, scope, implementation, verification evidence, limitations, screenshots and repository/demo links before publication.
- No invented clients, testimonials, revenue, quantified impact or accreditations.

## Candidate demonstrations (proposed; not yet published)

- Knowledge retrieval prototype: source-grounded search with traceable citations and failure reporting.
- Workflow automation prototype: a small process with approval gates, logging and recoverable failures.
- Research evidence workflow: a reproducible process from question formulation through evidence matrix to defensible findings.
